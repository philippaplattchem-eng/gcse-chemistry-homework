Module 1: Development of Practical Skills ▼
1.1 Planning
HypothesisA testable prediction, based on scientific reasoning, about the outcome of an experiment
Independent variableThe variable that is deliberately changed by the experimenter
Dependent variableThe variable that is measured in the experiment; expected to change as the independent variable changes
Control variableA variable that is kept constant throughout the experiment to ensure a fair test
Qualitative observationA description of what is observed without numerical measurement (e.g. colour change, precipitate formation, gas evolution)
Quantitative measurementA numerical measurement of a physical quantity expressed with appropriate units
1.2 Accuracy, Precision and Error
AccuracyHow close a measured value is to the true (accepted) value
PrecisionHow close repeated measurements are to each other, regardless of whether they are close to the true value
RepeatabilityThe closeness of agreement between repeated measurements made by the same person, with the same equipment, under the same conditions
ReproducibilityThe closeness of agreement between results obtained by different people, using different equipment, or at different times
ValidityWhether an experiment actually measures what it is designed to measure; a valid result is obtained under controlled conditions using an appropriate method
ReliabilityA measure of the consistency of results across repeated experiments; reliable results are both repeatable and reproducible
Random errorAn unpredictable, non-systematic variation in measured values that scatters results around the true value; reduced by taking many repeats and averaging
Systematic errorA consistent, reproducible error that shifts all measurements by the same amount in the same direction; cannot be reduced by repeating measurements
Zero errorA type of systematic error in which an instrument gives a non-zero reading when it should read zero
Anomalous resultA data point that does not fit the expected pattern; identified and justified before being excluded from analysis
1.3 Uncertainty
UncertaintyThe range within which the true value of a measurement is expected to lie; inherent in every measurement
Absolute uncertaintyThe uncertainty expressed in the same units as the measurement (e.g. 25.00 ± 0.05 cm3)
Percentage uncertainty(absolute uncertainty ÷ measured value) × 100%; used to compare the precision of different measurements
ResolutionThe smallest change in a quantity that an instrument can detect; the absolute uncertainty for a single reading is typically half the smallest scale division
Concordant resultsRepeat measurements that agree with each other to within a specified range (typically ±0.10 cm3 for burette titres)
Mean titreThe average of concordant titration results; used in all subsequent calculations
1.4 Processing and Interpreting Data
Significant figuresThe meaningful digits in a numerical value; includes all non-zero digits, zeros between non-zero digits, and trailing zeros after a decimal point
GradientThe slope of a line on a graph: gradient = change in y ÷ change in x; used to determine rates, rate constants, and other quantities
Line of best fitA straight line or smooth curve drawn through or close to plotted data points to show the general trend
ExtrapolationExtending a line of best fit beyond the range of the measured data to estimate values outside that range
Calibration curveA graph of instrument response (e.g. absorbance) plotted against known concentrations; used to determine the concentration of an unknown sample
Practical Techniques: Key Points for Paper 3 ▼
Titration

Purpose: Quantitative acid/base or redox analysis

Burette reads to ±0.05 cm3; use concordant results (±0.10 cm3); swirl throughout; note endpoint vs equivalence point

Colorimetry

Purpose: Measure concentration of a coloured solution

Use a calibration curve from known concentrations; absorbance is proportional to concentration (Beer-Lambert law); select complementary wavelength filter

Thin-layer Chromatography (TLC)

Purpose: Identify components and check purity

Rf = distance of spot ÷ distance of solvent front; compare with known standards; visualise with UV lamp or iodine vapour

Gas Chromatography (GC)

Purpose: Separate and identify volatile organic compounds

Identify by retention time against standards; peak area is proportional to quantity; GC-MS gives both identity and structure

Reflux

Purpose: Prolonged heating without loss of volatile reagents

Condenser fitted vertically; vapour condenses and returns; used for esterification, hydrolysis, synthesis reactions

Distillation

Purpose: Separate or purify liquids by boiling point

Thermometer bulb at side-arm; collect fractions at desired boiling point; fractional distillation for close-boiling mixtures

Synoptic Cross-Topic Definitions ▼
Equilibrium and Thermodynamics
Dynamic equilibriumThe state in a closed system where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction and concentrations remain constant
Le Chatelier’s principleIf a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change, the equilibrium shifts to oppose that change
Equilibrium constant (K)A ratio of equilibrium product concentrations (Kc) or partial pressures (Kp) to those of reactants, each raised to the power of its stoichiometric coefficient; value depends only on temperature
FeasibilityA reaction is feasible (spontaneous) when ΔG ≤ 0; ΔG = ΔH − TΔS
Entropy (S)A measure of dispersal of energy or disorder; increases when solids dissolve, liquids vaporise, or moles of gas increase
Acids and Bases
Brønsted-Lowry acidA proton (H+) donor
Brønsted-Lowry baseA proton (H+) acceptor
pHpH = −log10[H+]; a logarithmic measure of hydrogen ion concentration
KaThe acid dissociation constant: Ka = [H+][A−] / [HA]; units: mol dm−3
KwThe ionic product of water: Kw = [H+][OH−] = 1.00 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6 at 298 K
Buffer solutionA solution that resists changes in pH when small amounts of acid or alkali are added; contains a weak acid and its conjugate base
Kinetics
Rate of reactionThe change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time; units: mol dm−3 s−1
Activation energy (Ea)The minimum energy that colliding particles must have for a reaction to occur
CatalystA substance that provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy; not consumed in the overall reaction
Rate equationrate = k[A]m[B]n; relates rate to concentrations and the rate constant k
Rate-determining stepThe slowest step in a multi-step mechanism; determines the overall rate and the form of the rate equation
Half-life (t½)The time for the concentration of a reactant to halve; constant for a first-order reaction
Electrochemistry and Redox
OxidationLoss of electrons; increase in oxidation number
ReductionGain of electrons; decrease in oxidation number
Standard electrode potential (E°)The potential of a half-cell relative to the standard hydrogen electrode under standard conditions (298 K, 100 kPa, 1 mol dm−3)
E°cellE°cell = E°cathode − E°anode; a positive value indicates a feasible spontaneous reaction
Faraday constant (F)The charge per mole of electrons: 96 485 C mol−1; moles of e− = Q ÷ F
