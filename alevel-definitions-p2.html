Key terms from Module 2
Relative atomic mass (Ar)The weighted mean mass of an atom of an element compared with 1/12 of the mass of one atom of carbon-12
Empirical formulaThe simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound
Molecular formulaThe actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule of a compound
Percentage yield(actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100%
Atom economy(sum of molar masses of desired products ÷ sum of molar masses of all products) × 100%
OxidationLoss of electrons; or an increase in oxidation number
ReductionGain of electrons; or a decrease in oxidation number
Oxidation numberA measure of the degree of oxidation of an atom; the charge it would carry if all bonds were fully ionic
DisproportionationA redox reaction in which the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced
ElectronegativityThe ability of an atom in a covalent bond to attract the shared pair of electrons towards itself
Hydrogen bondA strong intermolecular force between an H atom bonded to N, O, or F and a lone pair on a neighbouring N, O, or F atom
Module 4: Core Organic Chemistry ▼
4.1 Basic Concepts
Homologous seriesA series of organic compounds with the same functional group and general formula, in which successive members differ by one –CH2– unit
Functional groupAn atom or group of atoms in an organic molecule responsible for its characteristic reactions
Displayed formulaA formula showing every atom and every covalent bond in a molecule
Skeletal formulaA simplified formula showing only the carbon skeleton and functional groups; C and H atoms are omitted
Structural isomersCompounds with the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements of atoms
StereoisomersCompounds with the same molecular formula and structural formula but different spatial arrangements of atoms
E/Z isomerismStereoisomerism arising from restricted rotation about a C=C double bond; E (opposite) and Z (same side) assigned by Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules
Optical isomers (enantiomers)Stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other; arise from a chiral centre
Chiral centreA carbon atom bonded to four different atoms or groups; also called a stereocentre
Saturated compoundAn organic compound containing only single C–C bonds
Unsaturated compoundAn organic compound containing one or more C=C or C≡C bonds
Homolytic fissionThe breaking of a covalent bond in which one electron goes to each atom, forming two radicals
Heterolytic fissionThe breaking of a covalent bond in which both electrons go to one atom, forming a cation and an anion
ElectrophileAn electron-pair acceptor; attacks regions of high electron density
NucleophileAn electron-pair donor; attacks regions of low electron density
Free radicalA species with an unpaired electron; highly reactive
4.2 Hydrocarbons
HydrocarbonA compound containing only carbon and hydrogen
AlkaneA saturated hydrocarbon with general formula CnH2n+2
AlkeneAn unsaturated hydrocarbon containing one or more C=C double bonds
Addition reactionA reaction in which two or more molecules combine to form a single product, with no other products formed
Substitution reactionA reaction in which an atom or group in a molecule is replaced by a different atom or group
Elimination reactionA reaction in which a small molecule is removed from a larger organic molecule to form a double bond
Free radical substitutionA substitution mechanism involving free radical intermediates; occurs in three stages: initiation, propagation, and termination
InitiationThe first stage of a free radical mechanism: radicals are generated by UV light causing homolytic fission
PropagationThe repeating stages of a free radical mechanism in which radicals are consumed and regenerated, perpetuating the chain
TerminationThe final stages of a free radical mechanism in which two radicals combine to form a stable product, ending the chain
Electrophilic additionAn addition reaction initiated by an electrophile attacking a C=C double bond
Markovnikov’s ruleIn addition of HX to an unsymmetrical alkene, H adds to the carbon with more H atoms already attached, giving the more stable carbocation intermediate
CarbocationA positively charged carbon species; stability increases in the order primary < secondary < tertiary
4.3 Halogenoalkanes and Alcohols
HalogenoalkaneAn alkane in which one or more hydrogen atoms have been replaced by halogen atoms
Primary (1°) halogenoalkaneA halogenoalkane in which the carbon bearing the halogen is bonded to only one other carbon
Secondary (2°) halogenoalkaneA halogenoalkane in which the carbon bearing the halogen is bonded to two other carbons
Tertiary (3°) halogenoalkaneA halogenoalkane in which the carbon bearing the halogen is bonded to three other carbons
Nucleophilic substitutionA substitution reaction in which a nucleophile replaces a leaving group
SN1 mechanismA two-step nucleophilic substitution: leaving group departs first to form a carbocation intermediate, then the nucleophile attacks; rate = k[RX]; favoured by tertiary halogenoalkanes
SN2 mechanismA one-step nucleophilic substitution: nucleophile attacks as the leaving group departs; rate = k[RX][Nu]; favoured by primary halogenoalkanes
Leaving groupThe atom or group that departs with the bonding electron pair during a substitution reaction
AlcoholAn organic compound containing the hydroxyl (–OH) group bonded to a carbon chain
Primary (1°) alcoholAn alcohol in which the carbon bearing –OH is bonded to one other carbon; oxidised to an aldehyde (then carboxylic acid)
Secondary (2°) alcoholAn alcohol in which the carbon bearing –OH is bonded to two other carbons; oxidised to a ketone
Tertiary (3°) alcoholAn alcohol in which the carbon bearing –OH is bonded to three other carbons; resists oxidation
DehydrationAn elimination reaction in which water is removed from an alcohol to form an alkene; requires a concentrated acid catalyst
4.4 Carbonyl Compounds (AS)
AldehydeA carbonyl compound in which the C=O carbon is bonded to at least one H atom (–CHO group); the carbonyl is at the end of the chain
KetoneA carbonyl compound in which the C=O carbon is bonded to two other carbon atoms; the carbonyl is within the chain
4.5 Analytical Techniques: MS and IR
Mass spectrometryAn analytical technique in which molecules are ionised and separated by mass-to-charge ratio (m/z); gives relative molecular mass and structural information
Molecular ion (M+)The ion formed when a molecule loses one electron in mass spectrometry; its m/z value equals the relative molecular mass
Base peakThe most abundant ion in a mass spectrum; the tallest peak, assigned an intensity of 100%
FragmentationThe breaking of the molecular ion into smaller ions in mass spectrometry; the pattern of fragments gives structural information
Infrared (IR) spectroscopyAn analytical technique that identifies functional groups by the absorption of infrared radiation at characteristic wavenumbers
WavenumberThe unit used in IR spectroscopy (cm−1); the reciprocal of the wavelength of the absorbed radiation
Fingerprint regionThe region of an IR spectrum below approximately 1500 cm−1; highly specific to each compound and used to confirm identity by comparison with reference spectra
Module 6: Organic Chemistry and Analysis ▼
6.1 Aromatic Chemistry
AreneA hydrocarbon containing one or more benzene rings
BenzeneA planar, cyclic hydrocarbon (C6H6) with a delocalised system of π electrons spread equally over all six carbon atoms
DelocalisationThe spreading of π electron density over more than two atoms; in benzene, the six π electrons are spread over the entire ring
Electrophilic substitutionA reaction in which an electrophile replaces a hydrogen atom on an aromatic ring, preserving the delocalised ring system
Friedel-Crafts alkylationElectrophilic aromatic substitution in which an alkyl group is introduced onto a benzene ring using a halogenoalkane and a Lewis acid catalyst (e.g. AlCl3)
Friedel-Crafts acylationElectrophilic aromatic substitution in which an acyl group (–COR) is introduced onto a benzene ring using an acyl chloride and a Lewis acid catalyst
Lewis acidAn electron-pair acceptor (e.g. AlCl3, FeBr3); acts as a halogen carrier catalyst in Friedel-Crafts reactions
Activating groupA substituent on a benzene ring that increases reactivity towards electrophilic substitution by donating electron density into the ring; directs to ortho and para positions
Deactivating groupA substituent on a benzene ring that decreases reactivity towards electrophilic substitution by withdrawing electron density; directs to the meta position
6.2 Carbonyl Compounds
Nucleophilic additionA reaction mechanism in which a nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbon of a C=O bond, adding across the double bond
Reduction of carbonylConversion of C=O to C–OH using NaBH4 (or LiAlH4): aldehyde → primary alcohol; ketone → secondary alcohol
Tollens’ reagentAmmoniacal silver nitrate solution; forms a silver mirror when warmed with an aldehyde but not a ketone
Fehling’s solutionAn alkaline copper(II) complex; forms a brick-red precipitate of Cu2O when warmed with an aldehyde but not a ketone
2,4-DNPH2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine; forms a yellow-orange precipitate with any aldehyde or ketone, confirming the presence of a C=O group
6.3 Carboxylic Acids and Esters
Carboxylic acidAn organic compound containing the –COOH (carboxyl) functional group
EsterAn organic compound containing the –COO– linkage; formed from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol
EsterificationThe reversible reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol to form an ester and water; requires an acid catalyst
HydrolysisThe breaking of a chemical bond by reaction with water
Acid hydrolysisHydrolysis of an ester using dilute acid and heat; produces the carboxylic acid and the alcohol; reversible
Base hydrolysis (saponification)Hydrolysis of an ester using aqueous alkali (e.g. NaOH) and heat; produces the carboxylate salt and the alcohol; irreversible
6.4 Nitrogen Compounds
AmineAn organic compound derived from ammonia by replacing one or more H atoms with alkyl or aryl groups; the N atom retains a lone pair
Primary (1°) amineAn amine with structure R–NH2; N bonded to one carbon and two H atoms
Secondary (2°) amineAn amine with structure R2NH; N bonded to two carbons and one H
Tertiary (3°) amineAn amine with structure R3N; N bonded to three carbons
AmideA compound containing the –CONH2, –CONHR, or –CONR2 functional group; formed from a carboxylic acid derivative and an amine
Amino acidAn organic compound containing both –NH2 and –COOH on the same molecule; the simplest units of proteins
ZwitterionThe ionic form of an amino acid in which –NH2 is protonated to –NH3+ and –COOH is deprotonated to –COO−; net charge is zero
Isoelectric pointThe pH at which an amino acid exists predominantly as a zwitterion and carries no overall charge
Peptide bondThe amide bond (–CO–NH–) formed between the –COOH of one amino acid and the –NH2 of another, with loss of water
6.5 Polymers
MonomerA small molecule that can react repeatedly to form a polymer
PolymerA large molecule formed by the joining of many monomer units
Repeat unitThe smallest structural unit that repeats throughout a polymer chain
Addition polymerA polymer formed by the repeated addition of unsaturated monomers (e.g. alkenes); no other products are formed
Condensation polymerA polymer formed from bifunctional monomers; a small molecule (usually H2O or HCl) is eliminated with each bond formed
PolyesterA condensation polymer in which repeat units are linked by ester bonds (–COO–); formed from a diol and a dicarboxylic acid
PolyamideA condensation polymer in which repeat units are linked by amide bonds (–CONH–); formed from a diamine and a dicarboxylic acid or diacid chloride
6.6 Organic Synthesis
Retrosynthetic analysisA strategy for planning a synthesis in which the target molecule is broken down step by step into simpler precursors, working backwards from product to starting materials
Protecting groupA group temporarily added to a functional group to prevent it from reacting during a subsequent step, then removed to regenerate the original group
RefluxHeating a reaction mixture with a vertical condenser so that vapour condenses and returns to the flask; allows prolonged heating without loss of volatile material
DistillationA technique for separating or purifying liquids based on differences in boiling point
6.7 Chromatography and Spectroscopy
ChromatographyA separation technique based on the differential migration of components between a stationary phase and a mobile phase
Stationary phaseThe phase that does not move in chromatography (e.g. silica in TLC; liquid coating in GC)
Mobile phaseThe phase that moves through the stationary phase, carrying sample components with it (solvent in TLC; carrier gas in GC)
Rf valueThe ratio of the distance moved by the solute to the distance moved by the solvent front in TLC: Rf = distance of spot ÷ distance of solvent front
Retention timeThe time taken for a component to travel through the GC column; used to identify substances by comparison with standards
1H NMR spectroscopyA technique using the absorption of radiofrequency radiation by proton nuclei in a magnetic field to give information about the number and chemical environment of H atoms in a molecule
13C NMR spectroscopyA technique giving information about the number and chemical environment of carbon atoms in a molecule
Chemical shift (δ)The position of an NMR peak, measured in ppm relative to TMS; reflects the electronic environment of the nucleus
TMS (tetramethylsilane)The reference standard for NMR; defined as δ = 0 ppm; gives a single sharp signal outside the range of most organic compounds
ShieldingThe effect of electron density surrounding a nucleus that reduces the effective magnetic field; shielded protons resonate at lower δ values
DeshieldingReduced electron density around a nucleus (e.g. near electronegative atoms or π systems), causing resonance at higher δ values
Spin-spin coupling (splitting)The splitting of an NMR signal into multiple peaks due to the magnetic influence of neighbouring non-equivalent protons
n+1 ruleA proton adjacent to n non-equivalent protons gives a signal split into n+1 peaks
Singlet / doublet / triplet / quartetNMR peaks split into 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 lines; produced when adjacent to 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 non-equivalent protons respectively
IntegrationThe relative area under an NMR peak; proportional to the number of equivalent protons giving that signal
High-resolution mass spectrometryMass spectrometry that measures m/z values to four or more decimal places, enabling determination of the molecular formula from the exact mass
