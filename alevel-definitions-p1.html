Module 2: Foundations in Chemistry ▼
2.1 Atoms, Ions and Compounds
Atomic numberThe number of protons in the nucleus of an atom
Mass numberThe total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom
IsotopesAtoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons
Relative isotopic massThe mass of one atom of an isotope compared with 1/12 of the mass of one atom of carbon-12
Relative atomic mass (Ar)The weighted mean mass of an atom of an element compared with 1/12 of the mass of one atom of carbon-12
Relative molecular mass (Mr)The weighted mean mass of a molecule compared with 1/12 of the mass of one atom of carbon-12
Relative formula massThe weighted mean mass of a formula unit compared with 1/12 of the mass of one atom of carbon-12; used for ionic and other non-molecular substances
IonAn atom or group of atoms that has gained or lost one or more electrons, giving it a net electric charge
2.2 Amount of Substance
Mole (mol)The amount of substance containing as many elementary particles as there are atoms in exactly 12 g of carbon-12; approximately 6.02 × 1023 entities
Avogadro constant (NA)The number of particles in one mole of any substance: 6.02 × 1023 mol−1
Molar mass (M)The mass per mole of a substance; numerically equal to the relative formula mass, with units g mol−1
Empirical formulaThe simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound
Molecular formulaThe actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule of a compound
Structural formulaA formula showing the arrangement of atoms and bonds, written out in a line without showing every bond
Displayed formulaA formula showing every atom and every covalent bond in a molecule
Skeletal formulaA simplified structural formula showing only the carbon skeleton and functional groups; C and H atoms are omitted
ConcentrationThe number of moles of solute per unit volume of solution; units: mol dm−3
Molar volumeThe volume occupied by one mole of any gas; approximately 24.0 dm3 at room temperature and pressure (RTP)
Percentage yield(actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100%; a measure of the efficiency with which a reaction produces the desired product
Atom economy(sum of molar masses of desired products ÷ sum of molar masses of all products) × 100%; measures how efficiently reactant atoms are incorporated into the desired product
Standard solutionA solution of accurately known concentration
2.3 Acid-Base and Redox Reactions
AcidA species that donates a proton (H+) to another species — Brønsted-Lowry definition
BaseA species that accepts a proton (H+) from another species — Brønsted-Lowry definition
AlkaliA base that dissolves in water to produce hydroxide ions (OH−) in solution
NeutralisationA reaction between an acid and a base to form a salt and usually water
OxidationLoss of electrons; or an increase in oxidation number
ReductionGain of electrons; or a decrease in oxidation number
Oxidising agentA substance that oxidises another species by accepting electrons from it; itself reduced in the process
Reducing agentA substance that reduces another species by donating electrons to it; itself oxidised in the process
Oxidation numberA measure of the degree of oxidation of an atom in a compound; the charge the atom would carry if all bonds were fully ionic
DisproportionationA redox reaction in which the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced
Half-equationAn equation showing either the oxidation or the reduction part of a redox reaction, including electrons
Ionic equationAn equation showing only the ions and molecules that directly take part in a reaction; spectator ions are omitted
Spectator ionAn ion present in solution during a reaction that does not participate in it and appears unchanged on both sides of the equation
2.4 Electrons, Bonding and Structure
First ionisation energyThe energy required to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous atoms in their ground state: X(g) → X+(g) + e−
Successive ionisation energiesThe energies required to remove electrons one at a time from a mole of gaseous atoms or ions
Electron shieldingThe reduction in the effective nuclear attraction experienced by outer electrons, caused by the repulsion of inner-shell electrons
Effective nuclear chargeThe net positive charge experienced by an outer electron after accounting for the shielding effect of inner electrons
Ionic bondThe electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions
Covalent bondA shared pair of electrons between two atoms
Dative (coordinate) bondA covalent bond in which both electrons in the shared pair are donated by the same atom
Metallic bondThe electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions and a ‘sea’ of delocalised electrons
ElectronegativityThe ability of an atom in a covalent bond to attract the shared pair of electrons towards itself
Polar bondA covalent bond with unequal sharing of electrons due to a difference in electronegativity, resulting in partial charges (δ+ and δ−)
Polar moleculeA molecule with a net dipole moment, due to polar bonds arranged asymmetrically
Van der Waals forcesWeak intermolecular forces present in all substances, caused by temporary induced dipoles
Permanent dipole-dipole interactionsIntermolecular attractions between the permanent partial charges on polar molecules
Hydrogen bondA strong intermolecular force between a hydrogen atom covalently bonded to N, O, or F and a lone pair on a neighbouring N, O, or F atom
Delocalised electronsElectrons not associated with a single atom or bond, but spread over several atoms (e.g. in metals and benzene)
Giant ionic latticeA regular three-dimensional arrangement of oppositely charged ions held together by electrostatic forces throughout the structure
Giant covalent structureA solid in which all atoms are linked by a continuous network of covalent bonds throughout the entire structure (e.g. diamond, silicon dioxide)
2.5 Shapes of Molecules and Ions
VSEPR theoryValence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion: electron pairs around a central atom repel each other and arrange to minimise repulsion, determining molecular shape
Bond angleThe angle between two bonds originating from the same atom
LinearMolecular shape with a bond angle of 180°; 2 bonding pairs, 0 lone pairs (e.g. CO2)
Trigonal planarMolecular shape with bond angles of 120°; 3 bonding pairs, 0 lone pairs; all atoms in one plane (e.g. BF3)
TetrahedralMolecular shape with bond angles of approximately 109.5°; 4 bonding pairs, 0 lone pairs (e.g. CH4)
Trigonal pyramidalMolecular shape with bond angles of approximately 107°; 3 bonding pairs, 1 lone pair (e.g. NH3)
Bent (V-shaped)Molecular shape with reduced bond angles due to lone pairs on the central atom (e.g. H2O, ≈104.5°)
OctahedralMolecular shape with bond angles of 90° and 180°; 6 bonding pairs, 0 lone pairs (e.g. SF6)
Module 3: Periodic Table and Energy ▼
3.1 Periodicity
PeriodA horizontal row of elements in the periodic table
GroupA vertical column of elements in the periodic table; elements in the same group have the same number of outer-shell electrons and similar chemical properties
PeriodicityThe regular variation in the properties of elements as atomic number increases across a period or down a group
s-blockElements in Groups 1 and 2, whose highest-energy electrons occupy s orbitals
p-blockElements in Groups 13–18, whose highest-energy electrons occupy p orbitals
d-blockElements in Groups 3–12, whose highest-energy electrons occupy d orbitals
3.2 Group 2 and the Halogens
Thermal decompositionThe breaking down of a compound into simpler substances by the application of heat
DisproportionationA redox reaction in which the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced
Displacement reactionA reaction in which a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from a compound in solution
3.3 Qualitative Analysis
PrecipitateAn insoluble solid formed when two solutions are mixed
Qualitative analysisAn analytical method used to identify which substances are present in a sample, without quantifying how much
3.4 Enthalpy Changes
Enthalpy change (ΔH)The heat energy transferred to or from the surroundings during a chemical reaction occurring at constant pressure
Exothermic reactionA reaction in which heat energy is transferred to the surroundings; ΔH is negative
Endothermic reactionA reaction in which heat energy is absorbed from the surroundings; ΔH is positive
Standard conditionsTemperature of 298 K (25°C) and pressure of 100 kPa; substances in their standard states
Standard stateThe physical state of a substance under standard conditions (298 K, 100 kPa)
Standard enthalpy of formationThe enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its constituent elements in their standard states under standard conditions
Standard enthalpy of combustionThe enthalpy change when one mole of a substance is completely burned in excess oxygen under standard conditions
Standard enthalpy of neutralisationThe enthalpy change when one mole of water is formed from the reaction of an acid with an alkali under standard conditions
Hess’s LawThe total enthalpy change for a chemical reaction is independent of the route taken, provided the initial and final conditions are the same
Average bond enthalpyThe average energy required to break one mole of a specified type of bond in gaseous molecules, averaged over many different compounds
CalorimetryA technique used to measure the heat energy change in a chemical reaction using a calorimeter; q = mcΔT
3.5 Reaction Rates
Rate of reactionThe change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time; units: mol dm−3 s−1
Activation energy (Ea)The minimum energy that colliding particles must possess for a reaction to occur
Boltzmann distributionA graph showing the spread of kinetic energies among the molecules in a gas sample at a given temperature
Collision theoryA model stating that a reaction occurs when particles collide with energy ≥ the activation energy and with the correct orientation
CatalystA substance that increases the rate of a reaction without being consumed in the overall reaction, by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy
Heterogeneous catalystA catalyst that is in a different physical phase from the reactants
Homogeneous catalystA catalyst that is in the same physical phase as the reactants
AdsorptionThe process by which reactant molecules bind to the surface of a solid catalyst, forming temporary bonds
DesorptionThe process by which product molecules leave the surface of a solid catalyst after reaction
3.6 Chemical Equilibrium
Reversible reactionA reaction that can proceed in both the forward and the reverse direction
Dynamic equilibriumThe state in a closed system where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction, and the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant
Closed systemA system in which no matter can enter or leave, though energy may be exchanged with the surroundings
Le Chatelier’s principleIf a system at dynamic equilibrium is subjected to a change in conditions, the equilibrium will shift in the direction that opposes the change
Equilibrium constant (Kc)An expression, at a given temperature, of the ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at equilibrium, each raised to the power of its stoichiometric coefficient
Position of equilibriumA description of the relative amounts of reactants and products at equilibrium; shifts left (towards reactants) or right (towards products) in response to changes

5.1 Reaction Rates (Quantitative)
Rate equationAn equation relating the rate of a reaction to the concentrations of reactants: rate = k[A]m[B]n
Order of reactionThe power to which the concentration of a reactant is raised in the rate equation
Overall orderThe sum of the powers of all concentration terms in the rate equation
Rate constant (k)The constant of proportionality in the rate equation; its value depends on temperature but not on concentration
Half-life (t½)The time taken for the concentration of a reactant to fall to half its original value
Rate-determining stepThe slowest step in a multi-step reaction mechanism; it controls the overall rate of the reaction
Reaction mechanismA series of elementary steps describing how reactants are converted into products, including any intermediate species
IntermediateA species formed in one step of a mechanism and consumed in a subsequent step; does not appear in the overall equation
5.2 Chemical Equilibria (Quantitative)
Mole fraction (x)The ratio of the number of moles of one component to the total number of moles in the mixture: xA = nA / ntotal
Partial pressure (p)The pressure that a gas component would exert if it alone occupied the total volume at the same temperature: pA = xA × Ptotal
Equilibrium constant (Kp)An expression, at a given temperature, of the ratio of equilibrium partial pressures of products to those of reactants, each raised to the power of its stoichiometric coefficient
5.3 Acids, Bases and Buffers
Brønsted-Lowry acidA proton (H+) donor
Brønsted-Lowry baseA proton (H+) acceptor
Conjugate acid-base pairTwo species that differ by one proton; the conjugate acid has one more H+ than its conjugate base
Strong acidAn acid that fully dissociates in aqueous solution (e.g. HCl, H2SO4, HNO3)
Weak acidAn acid that only partially dissociates in aqueous solution; the dissociation is an equilibrium (e.g. CH3COOH)
pHA logarithmic scale measuring hydrogen ion concentration: pH = −log10[H+]
Acid dissociation constant (Ka)The equilibrium constant for the dissociation of a weak acid HA: Ka = [H+][A−] / [HA]; units: mol dm−3
pKaThe negative logarithm (base 10) of the acid dissociation constant: pKa = −log10(Ka)
Ionic product of water (Kw)Kw = [H+][OH−] = 1.00 × 10−14 mol2 dm−6 at 298 K
Buffer solutionA solution that resists large changes in pH when small amounts of acid or alkali are added; contains a weak acid and its conjugate base in comparable concentrations
Equivalence pointThe point in a titration where stoichiometrically equivalent amounts of acid and base have been mixed
Half-equivalence pointThe point in a weak acid-strong base titration where [HA] = [A−] and pH = pKa
5.4 Thermodynamics
Lattice enthalpyThe enthalpy change when one mole of an ionic compound is formed from its constituent gaseous ions under standard conditions (exothermic; lattice formation)
Standard enthalpy of atomisationThe enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous atoms is formed from the element in its standard state
First electron affinityThe enthalpy change when one electron is added to each atom in one mole of isolated gaseous atoms: X(g) + e− → X−(g)
Born-Haber cycleA thermodynamic cycle based on Hess’s Law, used to calculate lattice enthalpy from other measurable enthalpy changes
Enthalpy of hydrationThe enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous ions is surrounded by water molecules to form a solution at infinite dilution
Enthalpy of solutionThe enthalpy change when one mole of a substance dissolves completely in water to form a solution at infinite dilution
Entropy (S)A measure of the dispersal of energy or the degree of disorder in a system; units: J mol−1 K−1
Entropy change (ΔS)The change in entropy: ΔS = ΣS°(products) − ΣS°(reactants)
Gibbs free energy change (ΔG)A thermodynamic quantity determining feasibility: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; units: kJ mol−1
Feasible (spontaneous) reactionA reaction for which ΔG ≤ 0; will proceed without a continuous input of energy
5.5 Electrochemistry
Standard electrode potential (E°)The potential of a half-cell relative to the standard hydrogen electrode under standard conditions (298 K, 100 kPa, 1 mol dm−3)
Standard hydrogen electrodeThe reference half-cell with E° = 0.00 V; H2(g) at 100 kPa over a Pt electrode in 1 mol dm−3 H+(aq) at 298 K
Electrochemical cellA device that converts chemical energy from a spontaneous redox reaction into electrical energy
Electrolytic cellA cell in which electrical energy is used to drive a non-spontaneous redox reaction
EMF (E°cell)E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode; positive value indicates a feasible spontaneous reaction
Salt bridgeA device connecting two half-cells that allows ion flow to maintain electrical neutrality
ElectrolysisThe use of electrical energy to bring about a non-spontaneous chemical reaction; oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode
Faraday constant (F)The charge carried by one mole of electrons: 96 485 C mol−1
AnodeThe electrode at which oxidation occurs
CathodeThe electrode at which reduction occurs
5.6 Transition Elements
d-block elementAn element in Groups 3–12 of the periodic table in which d orbitals are being filled
Transition elementA d-block element that forms at least one stable ion with a partially filled d subshell
Variable oxidation stateThe ability of transition metals to form stable ions in more than one oxidation state, due to the similar energies of the 3d and 4s electrons
Complex ionA species consisting of a central metal ion bonded to one or more ligands via coordinate (dative covalent) bonds
LigandA molecule or ion that donates a lone pair of electrons to a central metal ion, forming a coordinate bond
Coordination numberThe number of coordinate bonds formed between the central metal ion and its ligands
Monodentate ligandA ligand that forms one coordinate bond with the central metal ion (e.g. H2O, NH3, Cl−, CN−)
Bidentate ligandA ligand that forms two coordinate bonds with the central metal ion (e.g. ethane-1,2-diamine; ethanedioate)
Polydentate ligandA ligand that forms more than two coordinate bonds with the central metal ion (e.g. EDTA4−, which forms six coordinate bonds)
Chelate effectThe increased thermodynamic stability of complexes with bidentate or polydentate ligands, due to the large positive entropy change when monodentate ligands are displaced
d-d transitionThe absorption of visible light that promotes an electron from a lower-energy to a higher-energy d orbital in a transition metal complex, causing the complex to appear coloured
Ligand substitutionA reaction in which one or more ligands in a complex are replaced by other ligands
AmphotericA substance that can act as both an acid and a base (e.g. Al(OH)3 reacts with both acids and alkalis)
cis isomerAn isomer in which two identical ligands are positioned on the same side of the central metal ion (90° apart in an octahedral complex)
trans isomerAn isomer in which two identical ligands are positioned on opposite sides of the central metal ion (180° apart in an octahedral complex)
Optical isomers of complex ionsNon-superimposable mirror-image forms of a complex; arise in octahedral complexes with three bidentate ligands
